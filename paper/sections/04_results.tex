The Fisher Information Matrix (FIM)---the negative expected value of the Hessian---was evaluated using the real DESI BAO comoving distance residuals (12 measurements, 7 redshift bins, $0.295 \leq z \leq 2.33$). All five eigenvalues of the Hessian are strictly positive, confirming that the MAP point is a local maximum of the empirical likelihood surface. The diagonal entry $F_\tau = \partial^2(-\ln\mathcal{L}_{\text{BAO}})/\partial\tau^2$ evaluated at $\tau=0.50$ yields a Cramér-Rao lower bound $\sigma(\tau) \geq 1/\sqrt{F_\tau}$, consistent with the posterior width observed in the 300-generation MCMC chains.

To assess observational consistency, we processed 80{,}376 galaxy coordinates from the ESA Euclid Quick Release~1 (Q1) \texttt{MER\_FINAL\_CATALOG} spanning the Euclid Deep Field Fornax and Euclid Deep Field North tiles~\cite{EuclidQ12024}. Accordingly, no independent $S_8$ constraint is derived from this dataset.
