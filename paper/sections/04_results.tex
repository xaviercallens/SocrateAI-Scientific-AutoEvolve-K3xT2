\subsection{Bayesian Model Selection}
\label{subsec:bayes}

To rigorously compare the $K3 \times T^2$ model against standard $\Lambda$CDM, we computed the Bayesian evidence $\ln\mathcal{Z}$ using \texttt{dynesty} nested sampling with 300 live points and the \texttt{multi}-bound \texttt{rwalk} sampler.

Under informed multivariate Gaussian priors initially derived from the full 300-generation MAP posterior, the DESI BAO likelihood yielded log-evidences resulting in an artificially inflated Bayes factor ($\ln\mathcal{B}_{10} = 13.60$). To address this empirical Bayes circularity and prevent double-counting of the data, we implemented a strict prior separation algorithm. The MAP covariance was reconstructed exclusively from a training set (Generations 1–150), and the nested sampling was evaluated strictly against an independent test set (Generations 151–300).

Under this unbiased methodology, the evidence degrades significantly to $\ln\mathcal{Z}_{K3T2} = -30.06$ (a severe penalty of $\Delta\ln\mathcal{Z} = -22.68$). Consequently, the corrected Bayes factor strongly penalizes the $K3 \times T^2$ model relative to $\Lambda$CDM when proper statistical separation is enforced. This robust falsification highlights that the initial "decisive" evidence was an artifact of circular priors, and it underscores the necessity of anchoring the moduli tightly to first-principles physical constraints (like the Ap\'ery $\zeta(3)$ scalar potential derivations in Section \ref{sec:eft_bridge}) rather than relying on phenomenological MCMC parameter expansion.
